
Agricultural Commodity Price Intelligence and Forecasting for Farmer Decisions

Challenge overview
Develop a robust market-intelligence and price-forecasting capability that can help farmers make better short-term decisions on whether to hold, store or sell produce and longer-term decisions on which annual crops to sow or plant.
The Agriculture Department states that there is currently no mechanism for near-accurate forecasting of agricultural commodity prices in the State. This makes crop-planning decisions and post-harvest hold/sell decisions difficult. Daily spot-market prices and arrival information are already collected by the Agricultural Market Intelligence Unit (AMIU) of Assam Agricultural University through Agriculture Extension Assistants and made available to farmers, but price forecasts are not currently available. The department also notes that long-term historical market data are incomplete because of the unorganised nature of markets and poor data maintenance, creating a challenge for conventional forecasting approaches.
The intended users and operational stakeholders are primarily farmers and farmer-facing extension systems, with market-intelligence and agricultural planning institutions also having a role.

What the challenge is seeking to improve
The department is looking for a practical way to move from the current state described above toward the following improvements:
• Better short-term visibility of likely price movement to support hold/store/sell decisions.
• Better long-term price intelligence to inform crop selection and planting decisions.
• Reduced dependence on incomplete or poorly maintained historical market data where feasible.
• More usable market intelligence that can be communicated through existing farmer-facing channels.

What a solution should enable
A proposal does not need to follow a single technology path, but it should demonstrate how the following capabilities would be delivered in the stated government context:
• Develop a forecasting approach that explicitly handles incomplete historical data and explains the assumptions and sources used.
• Use currently available spot prices and arrival information and, where appropriate, other defensible data sources to improve forecasting.
• Distinguish short-horizon price forecasts for marketing decisions from longer-horizon intelligence for crop planning.
• Provide outputs in an understandable form that can support farmer decisions rather than presenting only technical forecasts.

Inputs and available resources
The department notes that AMIU of Assam Agricultural University collects daily spot market prices and arrival data through Agriculture Extension Assistants. The submission also identifies the Assam Agricultural University, Agmarknet and ARIAS Society among relevant resources. It explicitly notes that long-term historical price data for the State are incomplete; applicants should therefore explain what alternative or supplementary data they would require. The department suggests that comparable markets in other States and insights from futures commodity markets may provide useful clues.

Indicative pilot / demonstration context
Pilot implementation is indicated as feasible. No specific commodities, markets or districts are identified in the submission; these should be defined with the department during proposal assessment.

Potential for wider application
The department indicates potential for scale-up after validation, including broader use of the forecasting capability in farmer decision support.

Indicative technology directions from the department
Time-series forecasting, market analytics, alternative-data approaches, comparable-market analysis and other quantitative methods may be considered. The department specifically highlights the challenge of forecasting despite incomplete local historical data; alternative methods that address this constraint are welcome.
